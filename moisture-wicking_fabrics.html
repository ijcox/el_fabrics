Moisture-Wicking Fabrics

Moisture-wicking fabrics are engineered textiles designed to pull sweat away from the skin and move it to the outer surface of the fabric, where it can evaporate quickly. They are widely used in athletic wear, outdoor clothing, and performance apparel.

What Does “Moisture-Wicking” Mean?

Wicking refers to capillary action — the process by which liquid moves through small spaces in a material. Moisture-wicking fabrics draw perspiration away from the body, helping keep the wearer dry and comfortable.

Unlike absorbent fabrics such as cotton, which hold moisture, moisture-wicking fabrics transport sweat outward for faster evaporation.

How Moisture-Wicking Fabrics Work

1. Capillary Action

Specially engineered fibers create tiny channels that move moisture from the inside surface to the outside surface of the fabric.

2. Evaporation

Once moisture reaches the outer surface, it spreads out and evaporates more quickly.

3. Fiber Engineering

Some fibers are shaped (e.g., multi-channel or hollow) to enhance moisture movement.

Common Fibers Used

Polyester

Most common moisture-wicking fiber; lightweight and quick-drying.

Nylon

Smooth, strong, and moisture-resistant.

Spandex

Provides stretch and flexibility when blended.

Wool (Merino)

Natural moisture management with odor resistance.

Polypropylene

Extremely hydrophobic and lightweight.

Types of Moisture-Wicking Fabrics

  • Performance knits (see Knitted Fabrics)
  • Mesh fabrics for ventilation
  • Compression fabrics
  • Blended athletic textiles
  • Technical base layers

Key Properties

Property Description
Quick-Drying Evaporates sweat rapidly
Breathable Allows airflow
Lightweight Comfortable for athletic use
Stretch Often blended with elastane
Odor Control May include antimicrobial treatments

Common Uses

  • Sports jerseys and activewear
  • Running shirts and shorts
  • Yoga and gym apparel
  • Outdoor hiking clothing
  • Undergarments and base layers

Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Keeps skin dry
  • Reduces chafing
  • Lightweight and comfortable
  • Improves temperature regulation

Disadvantages

  • May retain odors without treatment
  • Often made from synthetic fibers
  • Can melt under high heat (synthetics)

Care Instructions

  • Wash in cold water.
  • Avoid fabric softeners (they reduce wicking ability).
  • Air dry or tumble dry low.
  • Avoid high heat ironing.
