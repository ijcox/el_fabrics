Wool
A Complete Guide to the Natural Animal Fiber

Overview

Wool is a natural protein fiber obtained primarily from the fleece of sheep, though it can also come from goats, alpacas, rabbits, and other animals. It is valued for its insulation, elasticity, durability, and moisture-wicking properties.

Wool is widely used in clothing, blankets, carpets, upholstery, and industrial materials. Its unique structure makes it one of the most versatile and high-performance natural fibers.

History of Wool

Wool has been used for thousands of years and was one of the first fibers spun into yarn.

  • Domesticated sheep were raised for wool as early as 10,000 BCE.
  • Wool trade played a major role in medieval European economies.
  • The Industrial Revolution mechanized wool spinning and weaving.

How Wool Is Produced

  1. Shearing (removing fleece from animals)
  2. Scouring (cleaning the raw wool)
  3. Carding (aligning fibers)
  4. Spinning into yarn
  5. Weaving or knitting into fabric

Properties of Wool

Property Description
Insulation Traps air within fibers, retaining heat efficiently.
Moisture-Wicking Absorbs moisture vapor while keeping the surface dry.
Elasticity Can stretch and return to shape without breaking.
Flame Resistance Naturally flame-retardant compared to synthetic fibers.
Biodegradability Decomposes naturally in soil.

Types of Wool

Merino Wool

Fine, soft fibers commonly used in performance clothing.

Cashmere

Luxurious and soft wool from goat undercoats.

Alpaca Wool

Lightweight and hypoallergenic fiber from alpacas.

Lambswool

Wool from a lamb’s first shearing; very soft texture.

Wool Compared to Other Fabrics

  • Cotton – More breathable in hot weather but less insulating.
  • Linen – Cooler and lighter but not insulating.
  • Silk – Smoother and lighter but less durable.
  • Polyester – Synthetic, wrinkle-resistant alternative.

Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Excellent insulation
  • Naturally odor-resistant
  • Durable and elastic
  • Flame-resistant

Disadvantages

  • Can shrink if improperly washed
  • May feel itchy to sensitive skin
  • Typically more expensive

Care Instructions

  • Hand wash or use wool cycle on washing machines.
  • Use cold water and gentle detergent.
  • Lay flat to dry to prevent stretching.
  • Avoid high heat to prevent shrinking.

Environmental Impact

Wool is renewable and biodegradable, but livestock farming has environmental impacts such as land use and methane emissions. Sustainable wool farming practices aim to reduce ecological footprint and improve animal welfare.
