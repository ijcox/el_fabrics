Polypropylene
Polypropylene is a versatile synthetic fiber and thermoplastic widely used in textiles, packaging, and industrial applications.

Polypropylene (PP) is a polymer made from propylene monomers. It is lightweight, chemically resistant, and has excellent durability. As a fabric, it is hydrophobic, meaning it does not absorb water, making it ideal for moisture-wicking and outdoor applications.

Polypropylene is commonly used in moisture-wicking fabrics, ropes, geotextiles, packaging, automotive components, and household goods.

History

Polypropylene was first polymerized in 1954 by Giulio Natta and Paul Hogan. Its introduction revolutionized plastics and fibers by providing a lightweight, strong, and chemically resistant material.

Key Properties

Property Description
Lightweight Lower density than many other plastics and fibers
Hydrophobic Does not absorb water; ideal for moisture-wicking and outdoor use
Chemical Resistance Resistant to acids, bases, and many solvents
Durability High abrasion resistance and long lifespan
Flexibility Can be woven into fabrics or molded into hard plastic components

Applications

  • Moisture-wicking and activewear fabrics
  • Ropes, geotextiles, and carpets
  • Packaging materials such as containers and bottles
  • Automotive components
  • Medical and industrial equipment

Care and Handling

  • Wash with mild detergents; polypropylene fabrics dry quickly
  • Avoid high heat as PP has a relatively low melting point (~160°C)
  • Store in dry conditions away from prolonged sunlight for long-term durability
