Fabrics

Fabrics are flexible materials made by weaving, knitting, felting, bonding, or otherwise interlacing natural or synthetic fibers. They are essential in clothing, upholstery, industrial applications, and technical textiles.

Natural Fabrics

Cotton

Soft, breathable fiber from the cotton plant; widely used in everyday clothing.

Linen

Made from flax fibers; lightweight and highly breathable.

Wool

Animal fiber known for warmth and insulation properties.

Silk

Luxurious fiber produced by silkworms; smooth and lustrous.

Hemp

Durable plant-based fiber known for sustainability and strength.

Synthetic Fabrics

Polyester

Durable, wrinkle-resistant synthetic fiber widely used in clothing.

Nylon

Strong, elastic synthetic fabric often used in activewear.

Spandex

Highly elastic fiber commonly blended into stretch garments.

Acrylic

Lightweight synthetic alternative to wool.

Rayon

Semi-synthetic fiber derived from cellulose.

Blended Fabrics

Polyester-Cotton

Combines durability of polyester with softness of cotton.

Wool Blends

Improves flexibility and reduces shrinkage in wool fabrics.

Fabric Construction Types

Woven Fabrics

Made by interlacing warp and weft yarns.

Knitted Fabrics

Created by interlooping yarns; stretchy and flexible.

Nonwoven Fabrics

Bonded or felted fibers without weaving or knitting.

Specialty Fabrics

Denim

Durable cotton twill fabric used for jeans.

Velvet

Soft fabric with a dense pile surface.

Chiffon

Lightweight, sheer fabric often used in formal wear.

Leather

Material made from animal hide, used in fashion and upholstery.

Technical & Performance Fabrics

Moisture-Wicking Fabrics

Designed to draw sweat away from the body.

Waterproof Fabrics

Repel water for outdoor and protective clothing.

Flame-Resistant Fabrics

Engineered for fire protection and industrial safety.
