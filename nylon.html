Nylon
A Strong and Versatile Synthetic Fiber

Overview

Nylon is a synthetic polymer fiber first developed in the 1930s. It was the world’s first fully synthetic fiber and quickly became popular due to its strength, elasticity, and resistance to wear.

Nylon is widely used in clothing, hosiery, activewear, ropes, parachutes, carpets, and industrial applications. It is often blended with fibers such as Cotton or Polyester to enhance durability and stretch.

History of Nylon

Nylon was developed in 1935 by chemist Wallace Carothers at DuPont. It was first introduced commercially in women’s stockings in 1939.

  • Used in World War II for parachutes and ropes.
  • Replaced silk in many applications due to cost and durability.
  • Became a staple in sportswear and outdoor gear.

How Nylon Is Produced

  1. Chemical synthesis of polymer chains (polyamides)
  2. Melting and extrusion into filaments
  3. Cooling and stretching fibers
  4. Spinning into yarn
  5. Weaving or knitting into fabric

Properties of Nylon

Property Description
Strength Very strong and abrasion-resistant.
Elasticity Highly stretchable and retains shape.
Lightweight Strong yet relatively light.
Moisture Resistance Dries quickly and resists mildew.
Smooth Texture Silky feel similar to Silk.

Common Uses of Nylon

Activewear

Stretchy and durable fabric ideal for sports clothing.

Outerwear

Water-resistant jackets and windbreakers.

Industrial Applications

Used in ropes, nets, and safety equipment.

Carpets & Upholstery

Durable and stain-resistant flooring materials.

Nylon Compared to Other Fabrics

  • Polyester – Similar synthetic fiber but slightly less elastic.
  • Cotton – More breathable but less durable.
  • Hemp – Natural and eco-friendly alternative.
  • Wool – Provides insulation; nylon excels in strength.

Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages

  • Extremely strong and durable
  • Lightweight and elastic
  • Resistant to abrasion
  • Quick drying

Disadvantages

  • Petroleum-based
  • Less breathable than natural fibers
  • Can melt under high heat

Care Instructions

  • Machine wash in cool or warm water.
  • Tumble dry on low heat.
  • Avoid high heat ironing.
  • Do not bleach unless specified safe.

Environmental Impact

Nylon is petroleum-based and non-biodegradable. Its production can be energy-intensive, and it may contribute to microplastic pollution. Recycling technologies and bio-based nylons are being developed to reduce environmental impact.
