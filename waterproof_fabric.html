Waterproof Fabrics
Waterproof fabrics are textiles engineered to prevent water penetration while often maintaining breathability and flexibility.

Waterproof fabrics are designed to block liquid water from passing through the material. They are widely used in outdoor gear, protective clothing, industrial applications, and performance sportswear. Waterproof fabrics differ from water-resistant fabrics in that they provide a significantly higher level of protection against water penetration.

Many waterproof fabrics combine a base textile (such as Polyester, Nylon, or Cotton) with a waterproof coating or membrane.

How Waterproof Fabrics Work

1. Coatings

A waterproof coating is applied to the fabric surface. Common coatings include:

  • Polyurethane (PU)
  • PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)
  • Acrylic coatings

2. Membranes

Waterproof membranes are thin layers bonded to fabric. These membranes may be:

  • Microporous (tiny pores block water droplets but allow vapor)
  • Hydrophilic (solid layer that transports moisture vapor chemically)

3. Laminated Construction

Laminated fabrics sandwich a waterproof membrane between fabric layers for durability and comfort.

Waterproof vs Water-Resistant vs Water-Repellent

Type Protection Level Typical Use
Water-Repellent Light splashes Light jackets
Water-Resistant Moderate rain Windbreakers
Waterproof Heavy rain / immersion Rain gear, tents

Common Types of Waterproof Fabrics

Gore-Tex

Microporous membrane technology that blocks liquid water while allowing breathability.

PU-Coated Polyester

Polyester fabric with polyurethane coating for affordable waterproof protection.

Oilskin

Traditional wax-treated cotton fabric used in maritime environments.

Rubberized Fabric

Fabric bonded with rubber layer, fully waterproof but not breathable.

Waterproof Ratings

Waterproof fabrics are often tested using a hydrostatic head test, measured in millimeters (mm).

Rating (mm) Protection Level
1,000 – 5,000 mm Light rain
5,000 – 10,000 mm Moderate rain
10,000 – 20,000 mm Heavy rain
20,000+ mm Extreme conditions
Higher ratings increase waterproof protection but may affect flexibility and cost.

Applications

  • Rain jackets and outerwear
  • Hiking and mountaineering gear
  • Tents and tarps
  • Marine equipment
  • Protective industrial clothing

Care and Maintenance

  • Reapply Durable Water Repellent (DWR) treatments when necessary
  • Avoid fabric softeners
  • Wash according to manufacturer instructions
  • Store dry to prevent mildew
