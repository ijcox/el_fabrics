Nomex
Nomex is an inherently flame-resistant aramid fiber used primarily in protective clothing and high-heat environments.

Nomex is a meta-aramid fiber developed by DuPont. Unlike some treated fabrics, Nomex is inherently flame-resistant and maintains its protective properties through wear and laundering.

It is widely used in flame-resistant fabrics, firefighter gear, military uniforms, racecar driver suits, and industrial protective apparel.

History

Nomex was developed in the 1960s by DuPont as a lightweight, heat-resistant, and durable fiber for protective applications. It became widely adopted in industrial, military, and safety industries for its superior fire resistance.

Key Properties

Property Description
Flame Resistance Inherently resists ignition and slows flame spread
Thermal Stability Maintains integrity at temperatures up to 370°C (698°F)
Durability Maintains flame-resistant properties after repeated washing
Lightweight Comfortable for clothing and protective wear
Chemical Resistance Resistant to many industrial chemicals and solvents

Applications

  • Firefighter suits and gloves
  • Industrial protective clothing
  • Racecar driver uniforms
  • Military uniforms and equipment
  • Electrical insulation and protective gear
  • Flight suits for aviation and aerospace personnel

Forms and Blends

100% Nomex Fabric

Used in high-heat protective garments and industrial suits.

Nomex Blends

Blended with cotton, Kevlar, or other fibers for comfort, strength, and durability.

Nomex Paper & Felt

Used for electrical insulation and industrial filtration.

Care and Handling

  • Wash with mild detergents; avoid bleach and fabric softeners
  • Air dry or tumble dry on low heat
  • Store away from UV exposure and prolonged sunlight
  • Inspect garments regularly for wear and damage
