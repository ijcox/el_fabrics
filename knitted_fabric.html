Knitted Fabrics

Knitted fabrics are a primary Fabric Construction Type created by interlooping yarns together. Unlike woven fabrics, which cross yarns at right angles, knitted fabrics form flexible loops that give the fabric natural stretch and comfort.

Overview of Fabric Construction Types

There are three main fabric construction categories:

  • Woven Fabrics
  • Knitted Fabric
  • Nonwoven Fabrics

Knitted fabrics are known for their elasticity, softness, and wrinkle resistance. They are commonly used in casual and performance apparel.

Types of Knitted Fabrics

Weft Knits

Yarns run horizontally across the fabric. This is the most common knitting method and includes jersey and rib knits.

Warp Knits

Yarns run vertically along the fabric. Warp knits are more stable and less stretchy than weft knits.

Jersey Knit

Lightweight and smooth on one side, commonly used in T-shirts and dresses.

Rib Knit

Features vertical ridges and enhanced stretch. Common in cuffs and collars.

Interlock Knit

A thicker, more stable knit fabric with smooth surfaces on both sides.

Key Characteristics

  • High stretch and flexibility
  • Comfortable and soft
  • Resistant to wrinkling
  • Does not fray easily when cut
  • Can lose shape if not properly stabilized

Common Fibers Used in Knits

Knitted fabrics can be made from various natural, synthetic, and blended fibers:

  • Cotton
  • Wool
  • Polyester
  • Nylon
  • Rayon
  • Spandex (for added stretch)

Advantages and Disadvantages

Advantages Disadvantages
Very comfortable and flexible Can stretch out over time
Wrinkle resistant May snag easily
Breathable (depending on fiber) Less structured than woven fabrics
Does not fray at edges May curl at raw edges

Common Uses

  • T-shirts and casual tops
  • Sweaters and cardigans
  • Activewear and sportswear
  • Underwear and loungewear
  • Socks and hosiery

Comparison With Woven Fabrics

Feature Knitted Woven Fabrics
Stretch High Low
Structure Flexible Structured
Fraying Rare Common
Comfort Very High Moderate
